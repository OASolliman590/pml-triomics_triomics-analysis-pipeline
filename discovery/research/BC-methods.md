## B-01 — When are paired deltas and subject blocking equivalent?

**Proposed answer / classification: inference with explicit algebra; fixed-effect blocking and estimated-correlation blocking must not be conflated.** For n complete two-occasion pairs, let delta_i = y_i1 − y_i0. Under an unweighted Gaussian model with a separate fixed intercept per subject and a common occasion effect, the occasion coefficient equals mean(delta). The raw-data model has 2n observations and n+1 independent coefficients: residual df n−1. A one-sample delta model has n observations and one coefficient: df n−1. Its residual variance is twice the raw-model residual variance, while its unscaled coefficient variance is half as large; the product yields the same SE and ordinary t statistic. With p estimable differenced covariates, both have df n−1−p. This is an algebraic identity under these assumptions, not an empirical equivalence claim across all settings.

An intercept-only fit of deltas is **not** a fit of 2n independent centered observations. Centered pairs are (−delta_i/2, +delta_i/2); there are only n independent differences. Ignoring their dependence creates incorrect residual degrees of freedom.

For `eBayes(trend=FALSE)` on identical feature sets, complete pairs, equal weights, and identical moderation settings, the common factor-of-two residual variance scaling should propagate through the prior and posterior variance estimates, preserving moderated t inference (up to numerical behavior). This needs an executable equality check. `trend=TRUE` is not automatically equivalent: `Amean` for a delta fit describes average change, whereas a raw-abundance fit describes average abundance. Missing pairs, unequal observation weights, different feature filters, imputation, changed covariate models or differing priors break the stated assumptions. Equality of coefficients alone does not establish equality of p values.

**Evidence / exact artifact:** [limma `R/lmfit.R`, RELEASE_3_23, `lm.series` and `gls.series`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bioc/limma/RELEASE_3_23/R/lmfit.R), inspected QR/SE/df computation and block GLS sections; [limma `R/ebayes.R`, RELEASE_3_23, `.ebayes` lines 35–65](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bioc/limma/RELEASE_3_23/R/ebayes.R), inspected inputs, shrinkage and t/df calculation. Release branches were inspected, not checksum-pinned source archives. The equations above are the researcher's derivation, not a quotation from these files.

**Counterpoint:** `duplicateCorrelation` estimates a consensus correlation and fits GLS, rather than n fixed subject intercepts. In a complete balanced two-level design with only occasion effects it uses 2n−2 residual df, conditional on the supplied correlation, and includes information from subject means in residual variance estimation. It is not generally the delta test. The limma author describes overlap and meaningful differences between the methods in [Gordon Smyth's answer, repeated-measure blocking versus correlation](https://support.bioconductor.org/p/128704/), supporting fixed blocking when block differences are large and noting advantages of correlation models for unbalanced/confounded blocks.

**Uncertainty:** numerical moderation equality and weighted/missing-data limits unrun. **Specification effect:** require distinct names for fixed subject effects, delta analysis and consensus-correlation GLS; preserve estimand, pair order and residual df in output provenance. **Decision status:** factual/algebraic challenge documented; modeling choice and ADR pending.

## B-02 — Is subject-centering followed by duplicateCorrelation valid for >2 visits?

**Proposed answer / classification: inference exposing an unresolved statistical contract.** Let a subject have k visits and C = I − 11'/k. Centering changes independent residual covariance sigma²I into sigma²C, which has rank k−1. Off-diagonal correlations become −1/(k−1), even when the original measurements had no serial dependence. For a random-intercept model, centering removes the random intercept but leaves this constrained covariance. Each subject's centered residuals sum to zero. For balanced k visits with a full occasion contrast model, effective residual df is (n−1)(k−1), not the nk−k reported by an ordinary model that does not account for the n centering constraints. Subtracting subject means from the response also requires transforming relevant design columns; centering the response alone does not justify an arbitrary untransformed covariate model.

**Evidence / exact artifact:** [limma reference manual, `dupcor` / `duplicateCorrelation`, Details](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/manuals/limma/man/limma.pdf) documents per-feature REML and consensus correlation, with at least two more observations than design rank needed for useful estimation. The source-inspected `gls.series` in B-01 constructs compound-symmetric within-block covariance and uses Cholesky whitening; it does not incorporate an external subject-centering projection's rank loss. The rank/covariance equations above are derived here. [Liquet et al. 2012, Methods, multilevel approach](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3627901/), DOI **10.1186/1471-2105-13-325**, supports within/between decomposition for multivariate analysis, not this particular centered-data limma workflow.

**Uncertainty:** behavior at the singular boundary, unbalanced visits and feature-specific NAs must be measured. One cannot conclude that all centered-data inference is impossible; it requires a valid transformed model with correct rank/covariance bookkeeping. A spline basis encodes a mean trajectory, not a residual covariance model, so “limma splines” alone does not solve repeated-measure dependence.

**Specification effect:** the resolver row “subject-centred + duplicateCorrelation” must remain under challenge before implementation. Candidate ADR options include a mathematically valid contrast-coordinate fit retaining decomposition provenance, or an explicit change to branch representation/model handling. Neither option is selected here; both must honor the shared-matrix decision or seek its explicit amendment. **Decision status:** unresolved methodology / implementation gate.

## B-03 — Do shared decomposed matrices guarantee the same signal in inference and integration?

**Proposed answer / classification: inference, with the settled shared-input principle preserved.** A common matrix establishes common transformation provenance; it does not make method objectives identical. A mean-delta t test targets nonzero average change; covariance/factor methods target variation and supervised methods target association with an outcome. A constant nonzero delta across subjects can be highly compelling for a mean-change test yet be removed by feature centering before PCA/MOFA. Conversely, coordinated subject-specific deltas can have mean zero and still define a factor. Equality of matrices therefore cannot imply equality of selected features or biological interpretations.

Paired sample-level within matrices have 2n rows while delta matrices have n rows. A delta row is a subject/contrast, not an original sample. A single one-group delta study has no varying occasion label left to feed `block.splsda`; fabricating baseline/follow-up labels on delta rows would be invalid. DIABLO on the 2n within rows asks a different prediction question from unsupervised integration on n deltas. Correlation induced by the signed pairing structure needs an explicit null comparator.

**Evidence / exact artifact:** [MOFA2 FAQ, interpretation of factors and multi-group functionality](https://biofam.github.io/MOFA2/faq.html) describes factors as variation axes and states that group centering removes between-group mean shifts. [DIABLO official method overview](https://mixomics.org/mixdiablo/) describes categorical supervision. Matrix dimension and constant-shift examples are original derivations. **Uncertainty:** agreed historical meaning of “same matrices,” permitted deterministic adapters and score row identity must be reconciled by the orchestrator. **Specification effect:** define analysis units, estimands, projection/scaling provenance, label mapping, and behavior when a backend has no eligible outcome. Do not silently weaken the shared-matrix contract to “roughly related data.” **Decision status:** requires contract clarification and reviewed ADR if shape/semantics change.

## B-04 — What does limma moderation establish at small n?

**Proposed answer / classification: source-documented fact plus inference.** The inspected `.ebayes` calls `squeezeVar` on residual variances and df, divides coefficients by unscaled SE times posterior residual SD, and augments df with prior df, capped by pooled residual df. It errors when all residual df are zero. It does not create independent subjects or repair confounding, a wrong covariance model, imputation bias or selection leakage. Moderate n cannot be certified from an advertised sample-size heuristic.

**Evidence / exact artifact:** `ebayes.R` linked in B-01, `.ebayes` lines 35–65; [Ritchie et al. 2015, empirical Bayes section](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4402510/), DOI **10.1093/nar/gkv007**, explains borrowing information across features for small-sample inference. [Phipson et al., robust hyperparameter estimation, statistical methods and simulations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5373812/) establishes why hypervariable features can affect prior estimation and evaluates robust moderation.

**Uncertainty:** robustness, abundance trends and prior calibration on the intended transformed blocks are untested. Current limma can use a variance trend, so the broad statement “limma always assumes the same prior variance” is inaccurate. **Specification effect:** record prior/posterior variance, residual/prior/total df, trend covariate, robust option and exact contrast. Define whether table `SE` means ordinary or moderated SE; a mixed definition would make p/CI interpretation inconsistent. Treat robust/trend settings as configurable choices with diagnostics, not universally superior defaults. **Decision status:** backbone supported; settings and calibration pending.

## B-05 — What counts does DEqMS actually need, especially after differencing?

**Proposed answer / classification: documented fact with unresolved transformed-data extrapolation.** `spectraCounteBayes(fit, fit.method="loess", coef_col)` expects a limma eBayes fit with a feature-aligned `$count` vector. Its count-adjusted outputs differ from ordinary limma outputs; the adapter must expose the chosen method honestly. [DEqMS manual, `spectraCounteBayes`, Arguments/Details](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/manuals/DEqMS/man/DEqMS.pdf). [Official vignette, §2.1.5](https://bioconductor.uib.no/packages/3.21/bioc/vignettes/DEqMS/inst/doc/DEqMS-package-vignette.html) uses minimum PSM count within/across experiments; this is a versioned 3.21 precedent, not universal proof for every assay/count system.

**Evidence:** [Zhu et al. 2020, Materials and Methods; Results, dependence of variance on PSM/peptide count](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7261819/), DOI **10.1074/mcp.TIR119.001646**, validates count-dependent priors on studied labelled and label-free datasets. It does not establish that arbitrary spectral counts are interchangeable with quantified peptide counts or that this prior is automatically calibrated after subject decomposition.

**Uncertainty / inference:** Var(y1−y0) = Var(y1)+Var(y0)−2Cov(y1,y0); a single collapsed count may omit relevant precision information after differencing. Require observed variance/count diagnostics and calibration before claiming DEqMS superiority on deltas. **Specification effect:** count provenance, type, aggregation scope, strictly valid values and feature alignment are mandatory; missing count metadata cannot be fabricated or silently assigned one. Partial count availability and fallback policy need review. **Decision status:** documented API; paired/decomposed DEqMS calibration remains pending.

## C-02 — What is the actual withinVariation API and its scope?

**Proposed answer / classification: documented API fact.** `withinVariation(X, design)` accepts numeric predictors and a matrix/data frame whose first column identifies repeated individuals; it returns the within matrix, not deltas or a model. Additional design columns enable two-factor decomposition. [mixOmics 6.36.0 manual, pp.268–269, Usage/Arguments/Value](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/manuals/mixOmics/man/mixOmics.pdf). Manual metadata records RELEASE_3_23 commit **0f4d16c**, 2026-04-28. Direct source retrieval of `withinVariation.R` failed in this pass; its implementation has **not** been inspected.

**Evidence / precedent:** [official multilevel overview](https://mixomics.org/methods/multilevel/) and Liquet 2012 linked in B-02 support repeated-measure multivariate decomposition. They do not establish arbitrary-design validity. **Uncertainty:** singleton subjects, incomplete visit patterns, feature-wise NA means, two-factor semantics, and exact sum-to-zero behavior are pending checks. **Specification effect:** map IDs and design columns explicitly; perform decomposition exactly once under the resolver. No adapter should silently decompose already decomposed matrices. **Decision status:** signature verified in release manual; behavior verification pending.

## C-03 — What does the DIABLO design matrix trade off?

**Proposed answer / classification: documented method behavior plus retained user choice.** Predictor-block connections control emphasis on cross-block covariance relative to discrimination through outcome connections. Reducing between-predictor connections does not remove supervision; increasing them does not guarantee improved prediction. [Official DIABLO vignette, §6.3, tuning parameters](https://guides.mixomics.org/mixOmics-Vignette/id_06.html); [original authors' DIABLO repository, Parameters tuning and simulation discussion](https://github.com/singha53-zz/diablo). The manuscript presents data-driven connection choices as well as prior-knowledge choices, not a universally optimal value.

**Uncertainty:** the useful weights depend on objective, scaling, signal and sample size. A label “correlation” should not obscure the covariance-based fitted objective. **Specification effect:** retain the user's explicit-config/no-silent-default decision as a design choice; validate named block order, dimensions, finite range and symmetry/diagonal rules against the pinned implementation. Record the effective design after Y augmentation. If weights are data-tuned, their tuning belongs inside training folds. **Decision status:** user policy preserved; exact validation behavior and paper DOI resolution pending.

## C-04 — Does repeated stratified CV prevent subject leakage and stabilize features?

**Proposed answer / classification: inference; package support incomplete.** Stratification balances outcome classes, not subject identities. If one subject contributes rows to training and test, performance may describe recognition of a known subject rather than generalization to a new subject. Pair-centering before splitting individual rows also allows the held-out row to influence its partner's training representation. Whole-subject folds avoid that particular leakage; normalization, imputation, feature selection, scaling, design tuning and model tuning must still use training information appropriately. Predicting a new subject with all repeated measurements available is a different deployment target from predicting one future measurement from past information.

**Evidence / exact artifact:** [mixOmics `R/tune.block.splsda.R`, master, lines 125–157 and 455–478](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mixOmicsTeam/mixOmics/master/R/tune.block.splsda.R), inspected signature and dispatch. There is no `multilevel` formal argument in that source; `folds` is forwarded internally. The release manual's tuning Details documents stratified CV; it does **not** by itself prove grouped folds for repeated rows. [Official vignette, §6.3.3](https://guides.mixomics.org/mixOmics-Vignette/id_06.html) recommends more repeats for substantive tuning than its demonstration.

**Uncertainty:** arbitrary subject-preserving fold lists for the actual keepX path must be source/runtime verified; do not infer them from a different `perf` method. Repeats characterize split variability but do not create independent sample information or guarantee feature stability. **Specification effect:** nested grouped CV for performance claims; save fold manifests and leakage assertions; report selection frequencies, overlap and uncertainty rather than one “stable signature.” Any custom CV adapter must preserve established tool usage and be independently reviewed. **Decision status:** methodological gate; API/behavior check pending.

## C-05 — Are MOFA factors stable across seeds and factor counts?

**Proposed answer / classification: documented limitation and proposed evaluation policy.** The original MOFA training objective is non-convex. Its paper used random restarts and selected the highest ELBO rather than guaranteeing a global optimum. [Argelaguet et al. 2018, Materials and Methods, “Model training and selection”](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/statistics/statgen/joul_spr2024/6_Cartwright%20argelaguet-et-al-2018-multi-omics-factor-analysis.pdf), DOI **10.15252/msb.20178124**, inspected original paper hosted by a university (PMC direct open encountered a browser challenge). This is a method precedent, not a promise of current-release stability.

[MOFA2 maintainer source, `R/prepare_mofa.R`, `get_default_training_options`](https://github.com/bioFAM/MOFA2/blob/master/R/prepare_mofa.R) exposes seed, convergence mode, maximum iterations and factor-dropping threshold; the inspected branch defaults to seed 42 and no factor dropping. These defaults are not justified scientific choices for triomics. [MOFA+ 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7212577/), DOI **10.1186/s13059-020-02015-1**, is the later multi-group model reference.

**Inference:** changing factor signs/order is not instability. Align factors or compare subspaces before judging reproducibility; similar ELBO alone does not certify identical biological interpretation. **Specification effect / proposed choice:** record multiple seeds, ELBO/convergence diagnostics, active factors, aligned similarity and variance by view; expose initial factor count and pruning policy. Number of restarts and stability thresholds need justification, not copied paper defaults. **Decision status:** limitation established; evaluation thresholds and actual stability unverified.

## C-06 — What does “entirely missing views” mean for MOFA2?

**Proposed answer / classification: documented support with a necessary scope distinction.** MOFA2 permits samples missing some modalities, provided sufficiently many measurements are matched across modalities. [MOFA2 1.22.0 training vignette, §2 “Is MOFA the right method for my data?”](https://bioconductor.org/packages/release/bioc/vignettes/MOFA2/inst/doc/getting_started_R.html). It ignores missing likelihood entries rather than secretly imputing them during training. [MOFA2 FAQ, missing-value question](https://biofam.github.io/MOFA2/faq.html).

**Inference / uncertainty:** an assay missing for some samples differs from a view with no observed data anywhere. The latter supplies no evidence for estimating its loadings; similarly, a sample missing every view supplies no observed evidence for its scores. Disconnected overlap patterns can prevent identifiable cross-view alignment. Published support for missing entries does not resolve these degenerate cases. **Specification effect:** define missingness at entry, sample/view, full-view and full-sample levels; preserve observed/imputed masks; require overlap diagnostics. Negative subject-centered values imply a compatible likelihood (usually Gaussian), not untransformed count/Poisson semantics. **Decision status:** partial-view support documented; exact degenerate-case eligibility and runtime checks pending.

## C-07 — Does failure to recover a planted factor prove a software bug?

**Proposed answer / classification: inference; the older attached assertion is not defensible.** A planted factor can be too weak relative to noise, absent from informative samples, eliminated by preprocessing, orthogonal to DIABLO's supervised objective, or poorly identifiable among correlated factors. Sparse selection and non-convex optimization add uncertainty. MOFA's documented global-optimum limitation (C-05) alone defeats a universal recovery guarantee. The newer goal explicitly says synthetic data cannot substitute for real validation and absent expected effects must not automatically be technical failures; the orchestrator must reconcile this conflict explicitly.

**Proposed specification effect / choice:** separate (1) deterministic software invariants such as ID alignment, known transform identities, dimensions and seed provenance, (2) prespecified high-signal positive controls that should recover a clearly identifiable structure within measured tolerance, (3) null, weak-signal and adversarial scenarios whose recovery rates are measured, and (4) future real-measurement validation. Preserve failed recoveries as evidence; investigate without automatically calling them bugs. Compare integration with concat_baseline and univariate summaries on the same eligible subjects/features; evaluate held-out prediction when supervised, factor/subspace recovery when unsupervised, and incremental findings without promising superiority. **Decision status:** correction to older narrative warranted by reasoning and newer explicit guardrail; no recovery experiments run.
